Big Study Tracks Health

Australia has launched an 18 and Up study to follow 100,000 people over their lives to better understand mental illness and early-onset cancer.

  • 100,000 people will be recruited for Australia’s largest intergenerational study of wellbeing and ageing.
  • The study will follow people aged 18 to 90+ throughout their lives.
  • Almost two in five Australians aged 16 to 24 experienced a mental disorder in the past year.
  • Participants’ data may be linked to cancer registries and Medicare with consent.

Why it matters: Long-term evidence could help improve prevention, treatments and care systems for younger generations.

Healthwatch Gets Reprieve

Yvette Cooper has dropped plans to abolish Healthwatch England after a backlash from MPs, keeping the patient voice in the NHS.

  • Healthwatch England and its 150 branches will continue.
  • The body collects and analyses patient feedback on NHS services.
  • MPs from Labour, Conservative and Liberal Democrat parties had criticised the planned abolition.
  • Healthwatch has reported problems such as dental access and years-long ADHD waits.

Why it matters: Keeping an independent patient champion helps surface problems in care and supports better NHS accountability.

More GP Care Covered

More Australians are being bulk-billed for GP care after new federal incentives for doctors and practices.

  • 63% of Australians are now bulk-billed for all their GP care, up from 56%.
  • 26% of GPs reported bulk-billing all of their patients, up from 12%.
  • The report surveyed 2,766 GPs.
  • The share delaying GP care due to cost fell from 8.8% to 7.7%.

Why it matters: Lower costs make primary care easier to access, which can help more people get timely treatment and support.
